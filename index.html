Why it works

Six rules that make a developer accountable

Every one of them is enforced by the pad or the program, not promised in a group chat. Together they turn a launch from a gamble on a stranger into a trade on a record.

§3

Vetted developers

Three or more past launches checked on chain, a committee interview and 72 hours of public review. Nobody can buy a seat.

§1

Launches, announced ahead

Booked 48 hours ahead at a time the developer picks, and public at once. Liquidity meets a few tickers, not two thousand.

§4

Scored every day

Survival, holder outcome, peak and conduct. Inputs published with every write. Bottom two each season are open to challenge.

§6

Insiders disclosed

Every team and insider wallet listed before launch. Dev allocation capped at 5% and vested over 30 days. LP locked or burned.

§7

Break trust, get banned

Dev sells inside the vest or an undisclosed bundle: a permanent ban from the platform and the seat is vacated the same day.

§5

Everything on the record

Decisions, votes, bans, fines and every score input, in a public log and open JSON feeds anyone can verify.

Rules

How the pad works

Short, because they are enforced by the pad and the committee, not by goodwill.

§1

Twenty seats. Nothing else launches here.

Launch rights are a fixed supply of twenty seats. No seat, no launch. Seats are never sold; they are earned through vetting and kept through performance.

§2

Vetting

Every applicant goes through the same three stages before a seat is offered.

  • On-chain review. At least three prior launches with public wallets. No dev dumps, no undisclosed bundles, no linked-wallet sniping, no serial-deployer pattern across wallet clusters.
  • Committee interview. Identity is verified privately with the committee and never published.
  • Public review. 72 hours in which anyone can flag the applicant's history with evidence. Then the applicant joins the bench, ranked by pre-check score.

§3

No bond, one standard

Seats are earned through vetting and kept by ranking well on the public leaderboard. Nothing is locked and nothing is slashed. A developer who breaks the trust rules (§7) is banned from the platform for good, with every linked wallet.

§5

Seasons and replacement

A season lasts 30 days. In the last 72 hours the replacement window opens.

  • The bottom two seats at season close are on notice, as is any seat under 40 at any time.
  • Bench developers may signal a challenge at any time and file one inside the window with public evidence.
  • Holders of the incumbent's last three launches cast a holder signal, weighted by position, that the committee must address on the record.
  • The committee decides within 24 hours on the public record.
  • A seat on notice at two consecutive season closes is vacated automatically.

§6

Launch standards

Checked by the pad before a launch goes live, not promised in a group chat.

  • Dev allocation ≤ 5%, vested linearly over 30 days.
  • All team and insider wallets disclosed before launch and shown on the launch page.
  • No bundled buys in the first block from linked wallets.
  • Liquidity locked or burned at launch.
  • Slot booked ≥ 48 h ahead and shown on the calendar.
  • Public reports on day 7 and day 30.

§8

Collusion

Twenty seats could trade liquidity among themselves. The pad treats that as a trust break, not a strategy.

  • Weekly cross-seat clustering: seat wallets that share a funding source, or buy each other's launches in the first block, are flagged on the record.
  • Two confirmed links between seats: both developers banned and their seats removed.
  • Committee members may not hold any seat's launch above 0.1% of supply during their term.
  • Recall: 11 of 20 seats can remove a committee member at any time; the bench's top developer may not fill a committee seat.

§9

Fees and the seat pool

Every launch pays a 3 SOL launch fee when the slot is booked. It is forfeited if the slot is missed.

Seat pool · shared by all 20 seats pro rata by score at season close
50%
Treasury · committee, bounties, infrastructure
50%

A seat that finishes the season on notice forfeits its pool share to the treasury.

§10

Tenure

Three consecutive seasons without a notice, warning or fine make a seat tenured: one extra launch per season and a longer record on the leaderboard. One warning resets the count.

§11

Public review bounty

During an applicant's 72-hour public review, anyone can file a flag with on-chain evidence. A flag the committee upholds pays a 2 SOL bounty from the treasury. Flags without evidence are discarded; three discarded flags from one wallet and that wallet can no longer file.

FAQ

          
Straight answers

        

      

      

        
Why exactly twenty?
Twenty seats, a 60-launch ceiling per 30-day season and at least 2 hours between launches keep the pad small. That is few enough for every launch to get attention and liquidity, and many enough that no single developer can hold the pad hostage. The number is a constant of the pad, not a target.

        
Where do I buy a Twenty launch?
Where it launches: pump.fun, Raydium or whichever venue the seat uses, through any wallet or terminal you already have. Cadre is the calendar, the vetting and the public record, not an exchange. Every launch page here links to the chart and the venue, and the contract address can be copied with one tap once the launch is live.

        
Can a seat be bought?
No. Seats are earned through vetting and kept through performance. There is no sale, lease or transfer. A developer who stops performing is replaced by the next developer on the bench.

        
What happens if the developer breaks trust?
The developer is banned from the platform for good and the seat is vacated the same day. A ban does not repay holders and the token keeps trading; the pad does not freeze or delist anything.

        
Is the developer's identity public?
No. Identity is verified privately with the committee and never published. What is public is everything that matters on chain: the deployer wallet, every insider wallet, the score, the rank and the full conduct record.

        
How is the seat score different from a vibe check?
It is arithmetic. 30% survival (launches above launch cap at day 30), 35% holder outcome (day-30 holders at or above their entry), 20% peak performance (median peak multiple) and 15% conduct. Holder outcome weighs most because it is the one number a developer cannot buy. It is recomputed every day and the breakdown is on every seat.

        
Does a developer put money down?
No. There is no bond. A developer's stake is the seat itself: a place among twenty, a public rank and the launch-fee share. Losing it, or being banned, is the cost of breaking trust. The Protocol section shows every state a seat can be in.

        
What do the twenty seats earn from the pad?
Half of every 3 SOL launch fee goes into a seat pool that is split among all twenty seats by score at season close. Every seat therefore earns from every other seat's launches, which is the point: the twenty have a shared interest in the pad's quality, not only in their own tickers.

        
What does a bench developer risk by challenging?
Only time and reputation. A challenge needs public evidence, with a 30-day cooldown on that seat if it fails. Signaling a challenge before the window costs nothing and is public.

        
Who controls the committee?
The seat holders, one vote per seat, electing five members for one season each. The committee cannot create seats, sell seats or change the score formula; it decides vetting, bans, fines and challenges on the public record.

        
What stops a developer from launching elsewhere at the same time?
Nothing technically, and the pad does not pretend otherwise. But every deployer wallet and cluster is on record, so a parallel launch from a linked wallet shows up in the next review as a serial-deployer pattern, which is a trust break.
